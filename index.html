About Me

I am interested in designing and studying AI systems that support professional judgment while preserving human agency and independent capability.

My research interests grew from developing DealLens, an evidence-grounded human–AI decision-support system. I am pursuing two connected research directions: understanding how AI assistance shapes human judgment and long-term capability, and building conversational agents that learn from professional expertise and translate evidence into accountable action.

Proposed PhD Research

From Assistance to Authority

Calibrating AI Influence for Professional Judgment, Capability Growth, and Independence

A four-study human–AI interaction research agenda examining how AI advice and distinct AI roles shape professional judgment, epistemic authority, calibrated reliance, and independent capability. Proposed methods include think-aloud and retrospective interviews, an eight-week comparison of AI roles, repeated AI-off capability assessments, and experiments comparing continuous support, fixed fading, and capability-triggered adaptive scaffolding.

DealLens 2.0: From Evidence to Agentic Action

From Pivotal Evidence to Expert Memory, Reasoning Audits, and Actionable Professional Artifacts

A four-study computational and systems research agenda extending DealLens toward evidence-grounded conversational agents. The proposed work investigates pivotal evidence shifts within conversations, conversational expertise learning through human-approved agent memory, consequential differences between human and AI reasoning, and agentic generation of verifiable, context-specific professional artifacts under human control.

Featured Project

DealLens — Human–AI Decision Support for In-Progress Sales-Engineering Risk Sensemaking

DealLens is an implemented, role-aware human-centered AI research prototype connecting Salesforce opportunity data and Gong interaction-derived evidence with progressive risk monitoring, interpretable behavioral diagnostics, and evidence-grounded LLM guidance. Sales Engineers and managers can examine evolving risk, question AI interpretations, add contextual knowledge, and accept, revise, or dismiss proposed actions while retaining decision authority.

In six selected source-conditioned synthetic datasets, cumulative-mean logistic regression outperformed latest-call prediction (PR-AUC 0.878 vs. 0.827; Brier score 0.174 vs. 0.194). These simulated model comparisons do not establish improved human decision quality; a four-condition HCI user study is proposed to evaluate that question.

Publication status: First-author poster manuscript submitted to ACM IUI 2027 (under review); extended full-paper research draft available separately.
