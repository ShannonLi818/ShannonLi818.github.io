About Me

I am interested in designing and studying AI systems that support professional judgment while preserving human agency and independent capability.

My research interests grew from developing DealLens, an evidence-grounded AI decision-support system. I am pursuing two connected research directions: understanding how AI assistance shapes human judgment and long-term capability, and building conversational agents that learn from professional expertise and translate evidence into accountable action.

Research Interests

Research

From Assistance to Authority

Calibrating AI Influence for Professional Judgment, Capability Growth, and Independence

A four-study research agenda investigating how AI advice and different AI roles shape professional judgment, epistemic authority, calibrated reliance, and independent capability. The proposed studies examine judgment episodes, role-specific authority formation, longitudinal AI-off assessments, and capability-triggered adaptive scaffolding compared with fixed fading and continuous AI support.

Research White Paper (PDF)
DealLens 2.0: From Evidence to Agentic Action

From Pivotal Evidence to Expert Memory, Reasoning Audits, and Actionable Professional Artifacts

A four-study research agenda extending DealLens into an evidence-grounded conversational AI agent. It proposes detecting pivotal evidence shifts within conversations, learning reusable expertise through human-approved agent memory, auditing consequential differences between human and AI reasoning, and turning validated judgments into traceable, context-specific professional artifacts under human oversight.

Featured Project

DealLens — Evidence-Grounded AI Decision Support

A working research system integrating Salesforce opportunity records and Gong conversations to identify evolving decision risks and provide inspectable evidence and actionable guidance.

The predictive layer identified 82.5% of eventual losses by Call 4 and 93.2% by Call 8.
